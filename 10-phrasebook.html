Ionnoioni

Phrasebook

Everyday phrases for getting by in Ionnoioni. Each one gives the English, the Siddham, the romanized Ionnoioni and a literal back-translation, so the grammar is visible inside every phrase. These are tools, not customs: how speakers come to greet, thank and apologize to one another is theirs to decide.

Hello and goodbye

Hello!
𑖋𑖌!
aio!
literally: (the joy-greeting cry)
aio comes from the Primal Register, a burst of delight at seeing someone.
Good morning.
𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖨𑖿𑖡𑖸।
kre rne.
literally: good morning
Good evening.
𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖨𑖿𑖭।
kre rsa.
literally: good evening
Good night.
𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖨𑖿𑖭𑖲।
kre rsu.
literally: good night
Welcome! Come in.
𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖌!
kre tro o!
literally: good coming-toward
See you later.
𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖰 𑖝𑖿𑖃।
par kri toi.
literally: future see you
Go well. (to someone leaving)
𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸।
tro kre.
literally: walk good

How are you?

How are you?
𑖎 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖺 𑖝𑖰𑖌?
ka tso tio?
literally: how, you?
I'm well.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸।
nio kre.
literally: I am good
I'm at peace.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖰𑖭𑖰।
nio strisi.
literally: I am peace of mind
I'm a little down.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖝 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭𑖺।
nio ta sprauso.
literally: I am softly sad
And you?
𑖭𑖿𑖡𑖸 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖎?
sne tio ka?
literally: and you, question

Meeting someone

Who are you?
𑖎 𑖎𑖲𑖌 𑖝𑖰𑖌?
ka kuo tio?
literally: who, you?
How are you called?
𑖎 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖄 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖃?
ka kio u kio pra toi?
literally: what, they say you?
Ionnoioni has no noun for "name." You ask how someone is called, never for the name itself: a name is something said about you, not something taken from you.
I'm called Erena.
𑖄 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖊𑖨𑖸𑖡।
u kio pra noi Erena.
literally: they say me Erena
What do you call yourself?
𑖎 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖃 𑖝𑖿𑖃?
ka kio tio pra toi toi?
literally: what, you say yourself?
I call myself Erena.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖊𑖨𑖸𑖡।
nio pra noi noi Erena.
literally: I say myself Erena
I'm Erena.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖊𑖨𑖸𑖡।
nio Erena.
literally: I am Erena
No linking verb is needed: a name stands on the subject directly, like any other predicate.
Nice to meet you.
𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖰 𑖝𑖿𑖃।
kre kri toi.
literally: good to see you

Please and thank you

Please give me water.
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲।
nr, tre noi tsu.
literally: (gently) give me water
nr at the start of a sentence makes it an invitation rather than a command, which is how Ionnoioni says please.
Could you give me water, please?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲 𑖎?
nr, tio tre noi tsu ka?
literally: (gently) you give me water, question
Thank you.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖸𑖝𑖿𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖃।
nio tretra toi.
literally: I am gratitude toward you
Thank you so much.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖸𑖝𑖿𑖨 𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖃।
nio ra tretra ra toi.
literally: I am gratitude, intense throughout, toward you
You're welcome.
𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸।
kre.
literally: good
We help each other. (a warmer you're welcome)
𑖭𑖿𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖲𑖭𑖸।
struse.
literally: mutual aid

Yes, no, sorry and excuse me

Are you safe? Yes.
𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖎𑖿𑖨 𑖎? 𑖎𑖿𑖨।
tio kra ka? kra.
literally: you safe, question? safe
Ionnoioni has no single word for yes. As in Irish or Mandarin, you answer by repeating the verb.
No.
𑖡 𑖎𑖿𑖨।
na kra.
literally: not safe
To say no, negate the verb you were asked about.
Really. (emphatic yes)
𑖌𑖊।
oe.
literally: (decorative affirmation)
Sorry! (a small slip)
𑖝 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭।
ta sprausa.
literally: a twinge of guilt
I apologize.
𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖃।
pra sprausa o toi.
literally: I speak guilt toward you
Excuse me, may I ask you something?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖎 𑖝𑖿𑖃?
nr, ka toi?
literally: (gently) I ask you?
Excuse me. (to get past or interrupt)
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖡𑖿 𑖡𑖿𑖃।
nr, kran noi.
literally: (gently) allow me
Pardon and excuse ask to be released, not to confess, so they come from kran (allow) rather than from guilt.
Pardon me. (a small slip)
𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖡𑖿𑖭𑖿𑖃 𑖎?
skransoi ka?
literally: letting-it-slide, question?
I beg your pardon? (I didn't catch that)
𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖎?
pra pra ka?
literally: speak again?
Saying a verb twice means "again."
I beg your pardon! (indignant)
𑖎 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖎?
ka kio tio pra ka?
literally: what did you say, question!
ka at both ends makes the question insistent, held all the way through.
Will you forgive me?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖡𑖿𑖭𑖺 𑖎?
nr, skranso ka?
literally: (gently) letting go, question?

Questions and getting by

Where is the water?
𑖎 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲?
ka tse tsu?
literally: where, water?
Here. / There.
𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖀। 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖌।
tse a. / tse o.
literally: space with. / space toward.
Where are you going?
𑖎 𑖌 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺?
ka o tio tro?
literally: where-to, you walk?
How do I get to the garden?
𑖎 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖺 𑖌 𑖝𑖸𑖄?
ka tso o teu?
literally: what way, toward the garden?
When did she come?
𑖎 𑖝𑖭𑖿 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖌?
ka tas kio tro o?
literally: when (past), she move toward?
"When" is always two words: ka tas looks back, ka par looks ahead.
When will you come?
𑖎 𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖌?
ka par tio tro o?
literally: when (future), you move toward?
What time is it now?
𑖎 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖿𑖢𑖿, 𑖝𑖭𑖢𑖨𑖿?
ka krp, tasapar?
literally: what hour, now?
What day is it?
𑖎 𑖨𑖿𑖡𑖸?
ka rne?
literally: what morning?
Days are counted in mornings.
What is this?
𑖎 𑖎𑖰𑖌, 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖀?
ka kio, tse a?
literally: what, here?
Who is that?
𑖎 𑖎𑖲𑖌, 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖂 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖌?
ka kuo, kio i tse o?
literally: who, that one?
Why? / Why not?
𑖎 𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖸? 𑖎 𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖸 𑖡?
ka ske? / ka ske na?
literally: question because? / question because not?
How many stones?
𑖎 𑖂 𑖢𑖿𑖭?
ka i psa?
literally: question-number stones?
ka takes the place of the number, the way kan i psa is three stones. Ionnoioni has no other word for how many.
Do you understand?
𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖲𑖡𑖿 𑖎?
tio krun ka?
literally: you understand, question?
I don't understand.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖡 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖲𑖡𑖿।
nio na krun.
literally: I not understand
I don't know.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖡 𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖲।
nio na pru.
literally: I not know
Please say that again.
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖢𑖿𑖨।
nr, pra pra.
literally: (gently) speak speak
One moment!
१𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖿𑖎𑖿!
pan i prk!
literally: one second!
Can you help me?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖲 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖎?
nr, tio tru noi ka?
literally: (gently) you help me, question?
Help!
𑖃! 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖲 𑖡𑖿𑖃!
oi! tru noi!
literally: alarm! help me!
I'm lost.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖃𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖺।
nio soitso.
literally: I am a wrong turn
I need water.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖹 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲।
nio prai tsu.
literally: I need water
I'm thirsty.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖹𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲।
nio saitsu.
literally: I am thirst
I'm tired.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖰𑖭𑖹।
nio strisai.
literally: I am needing-rest
Okay. / Alright.
𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸।
kre.
literally: good

Money, distance and time

How much is it?
𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖎 𑖂 𑖢𑖿𑖭?
kio ka i psa?
literally: it, how-many stones?
Money is counted in stones, like the Roman calculi, the pebbles that gave us the word calculate. Context tells a stone in the road from the price of bread.
It's three stones.
𑖎𑖰𑖌 ३𑖢𑖿𑖭।
kio kan i psa.
literally: it, three stones
I'll pay you three.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖝𑖿𑖃 ३𑖢𑖿𑖭।
nio tre toi kan i psa.
literally: I give you three stones
I have no money.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖡 𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖢𑖿𑖭।
nio na pro psa.
literally: I not hold stones
Is it far?
𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖌 𑖨 𑖎?
kio ra tse o ra ka?
literally: it, there intense throughout, question?
ta and ra bracket here and there: ra tse a ra is right here, ta tse o ta is nearby and ra tse o ra is far away.
No, it's nearby.
𑖡, 𑖝 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖌 𑖝।
na, ta tse o ta.
literally: no, gently there throughout
It's right here.
𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖀 𑖨।
ra tse a ra.
literally: here, intense throughout
Right now!
𑖨 𑖝𑖭𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖨!
ra tasapar ra!
literally: now, intense throughout
It's coming fast.
𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖨 𑖝𑖭𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖨।
kio tro ra tasapar ra.
literally: it moves right-now
It's on its way, at its own pace.
𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖝 𑖝𑖭𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖝।
kio tro ta tasapar ta.
literally: it moves now-softened-throughout
ta tasapar ta works like Mexican Spanish ahorita: not now, not later, but when the time has come. Speed in Ionnoioni is relative: how "now" something moves.
I'll help you... ahorita.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖲 𑖝𑖿𑖃 𑖝 𑖝𑖭𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖝।
nio par tru toi ta tasapar ta.
literally: I will help you, now softened throughout
Please speak slowly.
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖝 𑖝।
nr, pra ta ta.
literally: (gently) speak gently, gently

Weather and directions

What's the weather like?
𑖎 𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖲𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸𑖭𑖿?
ka kio sutses?
literally: what weather?
sutses is the open sky taken as a whole world. ta sutses ta is fair, mild weather and ra sutses ra is a storm.
Will it rain?
𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲𑖢𑖸 𑖎?
par tsupe ka?
literally: future rain, question?
It's pouring.
𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲𑖢𑖸 𑖨।
ra tsupe ra.
literally: rain, intense throughout
ta tsupe ta is a drizzle.
I'm cold.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖡 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖺।
nio na kso.
literally: I, no fire
kso on its own is hot, ta kso ta is warm and ta na kso ta is cool.
Which way to the water?
𑖎 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖺 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖲?
ka tso o tsu?
literally: what way toward water?
Go straight ahead.
𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖸 𑖢𑖨𑖿।
tro o tse par.
literally: walk toward the space still to come
tse par is ahead and tse tas is behind. te is up and pe is down.
Then go right.
𑖭𑖿𑖡𑖸 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖌 𑖭𑖸 𑖢𑖨𑖿।
sne tro o se par.
literally: and walk toward the side still to come
se par is right and se tas is left, the way a written line runs from what is written toward what is coming.
It's ahead on the left.
𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖸𑖢𑖨𑖰𑖝𑖿𑖭।
kio separitsa.
literally: it, ahead-left
separitsa wore down in fast speech from tse par i se tas.

nio prai strisa.

𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖹 𑖭𑖿𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖰𑖭𑖲।
nio prai strisu.

Family, friends and love

Kinship words carry no gender: koa is a parent, kea a sibling, and te, pe and se point up, down and across the generations.

This is my parent.
𑖎𑖰𑖌 𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖊 𑖎𑖺𑖀।
kio nio e koa.
literally: she, my parent
My grandparent.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖊 𑖝𑖸 𑖎𑖺𑖀।
nio e te koa.
literally: my ascending parent
My aunt or uncle.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖊 𑖝𑖸 𑖎𑖸𑖀।
nio e te kea.
literally: my ascending sibling

Asking and consent

Ionnoioni has no word for yes: you answer by repeating the verb. When the question is about closeness, an unambiguous yes is that echoed verb intensified all the way through, ra ai ra! ("I want to, fully!"). Anything softer is not a yes: ta ai ta, nokotu (maybe), nio na pru (I'm not sure) or silence. Every request below opens with nr, which makes it an invitation, and closes with ka, which makes it a question.

Would you like to cuddle with me?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖋 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖺𑖭𑖸 𑖀 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖎?
nr, tio ai sprose a noi ka?
literally: (gently) you desire an embrace with me, question?
Could we hold hands?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖄 𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖭𑖸𑖝𑖰𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖎?
nr, u nio psetipro ka?
literally: (gently) we hand-hold, question?
pseti (hand) is psetui, tool + body, worn down by speech. psetipro is to hold hands.
May I kiss you?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖡𑖿 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖝𑖰𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖝𑖿𑖃 𑖎?
nr, kran noi pratipro toi ka?
literally: (gently) allow me to lip-hold you, question?
prati (lips) is pratui, speech + body. pratipro, lip-hold, is a kiss.
May I touch you?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖡𑖿 𑖡𑖿𑖃 𑖢𑖿𑖭𑖸𑖝𑖰𑖨𑖰 𑖝𑖿𑖃 𑖎?
nr, kran noi psetiri toi ka?
literally: (gently) allow me to hand-see you, question?
psetiri, from psetikri, is hand-seeing: touch as the way a hand perceives.
How would you like me to touch you?
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖎 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖺 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖋 𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖭𑖸𑖝𑖰𑖨𑖰 𑖝𑖿𑖃?

literally: (gently) how do you desire I hand-see you?

Making up

Ionnoioni keeps apart what English blurs. Guilt (sprausa) and commiseration (sprause) differ by one vowel, and the grammar shows whether the speaker owns the cause.

I'm sorry I hurt you.
𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖃: 𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖭𑖿 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖲𑖡𑖿 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖊 𑖍।
pra sprausa o toi: nio tas trun tio e au.
literally: I speak guilt toward you: I caused your pain
The accountable apology: guilt, and I as the cause.
I'm sorry; I didn't mean to hurt you.
𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖃: 𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖲𑖡𑖿 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖊 𑖍।
pra sprausa o toi: nio pr trun tio e au.
literally: I speak guilt toward you: I caused your pain, involuntarily
pr admits the act and disowns the intent. Honest when true.
I'm sorry you're sad. (sincere, no fault claimed)
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭𑖸 𑖀 𑖝𑖿𑖃: 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭𑖺।
nr, nio sprause a toi: tio sprauso.
literally: (gently) I am commiseration with you: you are sad
I'm sorry you got hurt. (the dodge)
𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭𑖸 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖃: 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖍।
pra sprause o toi: tio au.
literally: I speak commiseration toward you: you are pain
Worth recognizing: sympathy with no one causing anything. Ionnoioni has no passive voice, so the missing cause is audible.
I'm sorry you feel that way. (the dodge)
𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖻𑖭𑖸 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖃: 𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖭𑖺𑖨𑖿 𑖎𑖿𑖃।
pra sprause o toi: tio tsor koi.
literally: I speak commiseration toward you: you think so
Also worth recognizing: the feeling is recast as mere opinion.
I forgive you.
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖡𑖿𑖭𑖺।
nio skranso.
literally: I am letting go
Let's start over.
𑖄 𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖨𑖿 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖺 𑖡 𑖌 𑖡𑖡𑖿।
u nio par tro na o nan.
literally: we will walk from zero
