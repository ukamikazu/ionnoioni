Glossary (v3)
Organized by root class. Within each class, entries follow the language's own order: ai · au · oi · a · e · o · i · u · n · k · t · p · s · r

prinvoluntary / happened-to, spoken [prɛ], written without the vowel (same exception again). Echoes par (future): the action arrives at the subject rather than originating from their will. Together tr/pr closed a prior total gap: nothing else in the language marked chosen versus imposed action

e (possessive) has a second, more deliberate use beyond ordinary possession: reaching inside a multi-root kenning to re-possess just one inner piece of it (e.g. nio e prin tron, e saisau na pru = "my heart, its rhythm is uncertain"). This is distinct from i's job of binding a kenning as one whole unit; see the Idioms & Kennings page.

Connectives

WordFunction
sneand
srebut
steor
speif / then
skebecause (looks back from an effect to its cause)
ske otherefore (because + toward: looks forward from a cause to what follows)

"Instead" / "instead of" is built by recombining ste (or) with o (toward), not by negation: the unchosen option is never denied, only selected among. Two orderings carry different weight: ste o (forward-offering: "tea instead?") and o ste (retrospective-asserting: "walked instead of driving"). Left-binding rule: in every fixed pair ending in o (tas o, tse o, ste o, na o, ka o, ske o), the o belongs to the word before it, and two pairs never share an o. o ste is the one older exception. spe (if/then) also bundles both halves of a conditional on its own: a bare spe-clause followed by a second clause reads as the complete "if X, then Y" with no second connective needed.

No word for "name." Ionnoioni deliberately has no noun for a name. You ask how someone is called, ka kio u kio pra toi? ("what do they say you?"), or what they call themselves, ka kio tio pra toi toi?. A name is something said about a person, never something taken from them. Answering is simple: nio Erena, "I'm Erena."

After any relational particle, a pronoun takes its object form: a noi (with me), o toi (toward you), a u noi (with us).

Body (-ti the part, -tui the part at work, -kuo the part inside)

Body parts are named for what they do. A function plus -ti names the part (kriti, eye), and the same function plus tui (the physical body) names the part at work (kritui, seeing as a bodily act). The two began as one word, -ti being tui worn down by fast speech, and later drifted apart in meaning. A function plus kuo names the part inside, and with it the sense as it is felt. Outside words lean on their first syllable and inside words carry their stress inward.

Process-word families (cognitive/behavioral patterns)

General-purpose, amoral vocabulary for common things minds and bodies do, not clinical terminology despite being developed with real clinical concepts as a guide. These apply equally to the mundane and the severe, and to internal or external speech alike: krupri covers being absorbed in a hobby exactly as naturally as anything more serious. Each word's voluntary/involuntary default (or lack of one) tracks the real-world typical shape of that experience, not one universal rule.

Ideophones (exempt from the four root-class templates)

Ideophones imitate the sound they name rather than following animate, inanimate, verb or mood shape rules: the same kind of deliberate exception as kennings (length) and nr (sound-spelling). Sustained or buzzing calls lean on the trill (r); short bursts stay minimal, often bare vowel+consonant with no onset; calls with natural repetition may reduplicate.

Usage notes (common false-friend mistakes)

  • Plurals never fuse. "Friends" is u seu, two words, not seuu. The plural particle u always stands apart from the noun it modifies, the same as e (possessive) and i (attributive). If you find yourself gluing a particle onto a word's ending, stop: that's a different language's habit leaking in. Nothing in Ionnoioni ever changes its own shape to mark grammar; only the words placed around it do.
  • No vowel ever doubles. Because particles stay separate, you will never see two identical vowels collide into one long sound (no -uu, -aa, etc.). If a combination looks like it would produce that, the words have not actually merged: they are still two words sitting side by side, pronounced with a clean break between them.
  • Zero copula is not optional-sounding, it's structural. "Rock heavy," "you safe," "I love you" never take a linking "is/am/are" verb. Reaching for one is the single most common instinct to unlearn coming from English.
  • Negation attaches to exactly one word, never to the whole clause by default. na kra a toi (I am NOT-safe with you) and nio kra a na-toi-style scoping onto the object are different claims. Read na as pointing at the word immediately following it, not as a blanket "no" over everything after.
  • Mood words are not adjectives. A mood root standing alone after a subject is a full predicate (tio ai = "you are desire"), not a description waiting for a verb.
  • ra / ta / nr / tr / pr are the only particles that change meaning by position or carry a special sound-spelling exception. Every other particle (e, u, i, o, a, na, ka and the connectives) does the same job no matter where it sits relative to its target. tr and pr follow nr's lead: written without their vowel, spoken [trɛ] and [prɛ].
  • No ordinal/cardinal distinction. "Three" and "third" are both kan: position in a list or a date is read from context, not a separate word form. A bare numeral can also stand alone with no following noun, functioning as an implicit pronoun ("both," "the two") when context makes the referent recoverable.
  • Pronouns don't disambiguate between antecedents. kio/koi (he/she/it) carries no gender, number or class marking, so when two possible antecedents are equally available in one clause, nothing in the pronoun itself says which one it points to. This is a known, currently open gap, not an error to correct by guessing; where the ambiguity is genuinely harmless (often when the two nouns were already bound into one unit via i) it can be left to stand.

Notes for future coinage

  • Compounding before coining. Prefer combining existing roots over minting new atomic words.
  • Kennings are exempt from the 4-segment simple-root ceiling; ordinary vocabulary is not.
  • r/s convention (soft): r = intensify/active/urgent; s = gentle/settle/static. Also marks word class (verb = r-initial, noun = s-initial).
  • Final-vowel convention (soft, inanimate only, extendable as a vowel-shift heuristic to other roots whose meaning has a natural spatial/experiential quality): see above.
  • No phonemic glides, no vowel gemination, no case declension, no stacking affixes.
  • nr, tr, pr, ra and ta are the only particles with bracket (position-dependent) behavior; all others are fixed-position.
  • Written Ionnoioni records segments, not delivery: intonation is never notated.
  • Compound ordering tendency (enthusiastic suggestion, not a rule): a grounding/core element tends to come first, a qualifying/active element second: e.g. tuiion (self, then being), noikoi (being, then alarm-at-self).

A dedicated page, "Idioms, Metaphors & Kennings," covers how and why the language builds images like kre rna (good-dawn, excitement) or tso pru (way-know, proof) instead of coining atomic words, including when to leave a kenning unfused versus compressing it.
