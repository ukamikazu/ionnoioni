Ionnoioni

𑖂𑖌𑖡𑖿𑖡𑖿𑖃𑖌𑖡𑖰
"being and non-being"

Thank you for your interest in Ionnoioni ("being and non-being"). I was inspired to create this by the simplicity of Toki Pona, but where I diverged was toward a constructed language better suited to expressing emotions, which will become obvious as you read further. The primary reason, though, is that I happened to notice the N, O and I keys on my keyboard were more worn than any other key, and that gave me this idea. It was meant to be a fun exercise based on that, but it grew over the course of eight days, and here it is, newly born, for anyone to enjoy, and perhaps even help me coin new vocabulary and phrases.

What you will find here

This guide is deliberately layered, and you are not expected to read all of it before you begin. The alphabet, grammar and glossary that follow are enough, on their own, to read, write and speak Ionnoioni within a weekend. Where the guide goes further, into why a word is shaped the way it is, or how the grammar arrived at a particular rule, that depth is there for whoever wants it, not required of anyone who simply wants to use the language.

A small language, deliberately

Despite its accidental beginning, Ionnoioni settled into real structure: a small, closed set of sounds; grammar carried entirely by word order and a handful of invariant particles, rather than by declensions or endings that change a word's own shape; and a vocabulary built, wherever possible, by combining what already exists rather than by coining something new. A feeling as complex as trust, for instance, needed no word of its own, it fell out of combining the words for safety and belief. That principle, reuse before invention, runs through nearly everything in this guide, and it is the same principle I would ask anyone extending the language to keep in mind.

If you would like to help it grow

Ionnoioni was not built to stay finished. If you find a feeling, a relationship or a thing it has no word for yet, I would be glad to hear about it. The fuller notes at the end of this guide explain how new vocabulary has been built so far, root shapes, sound conventions and the habit of combining before coining, so that anything added continues to feel like it belongs to the same language, rather than a patch stitched onto it.
