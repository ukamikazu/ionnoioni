Numbers, Time & Calendar

The full numeral system, the time-word class and how they combine to state dates.

Numbers and time form a coherent subsystem of their own: not quite grammar, not quite vocabulary. This page walks through both from the ground up: the base digits, how magnitude and fractions are built from them, the phenomenological time-word class and how all of it combines to say something as concrete as a date and time.

1. Numeral Basics

The ten digits are atomic CVC roots, phonologically distinct from every other root class in the language (animate, inanimate, mood, verb, ideophone):

These Devanagari digits are the only form used to write a number in Ionnoioni. Number-words are never spelled out in Siddham letters, though the classifiers they attach to (below) are.

nan (zero) carries its own quiet weight, an in-world "void" origin.

Numbers attach to nouns using the existing attributive particle i, the same particle that links any modifier to a noun:

kan i psa "three stones"

Ionnoioni makes no grammatical distinction between cardinal and ordinal numbers: "three" and "third" are both simply kan. Position and context carry the ordinal sense when it's needed (see Day-of-Month, below).

2. The Magnitude Ladder

An earlier approach multiplied upward by repeating seo (seo seo seo... for thousands), but this scales badly: the bigger the number, the longer and clumsier the phrase became. The system was reworked around one guiding principle:

Design principle Bigger magnitudes should sound lighter, not heavier. The words you reach for most relative to their size should be the most efficient to say, the way frequently used words erode and shorten in natural languages.

That gives six magnitude words, each one a further compression rather than a further repetition:

MagnitudeWordNote
×10seobase multiplier
×100 (hundred)seseopartial reduplication of seo, "seo of seo"
×1,000 (thousand)sesocompressed further, shorter than hundred despite being bigger
×1,000,000 (million)raseor = intensifier, applied to the multiplier itself
×1,000,000,000 (billion)oiseooi = alarm (founding mood primal)
×1,000,000,000,000 (trillion)auseoau = pain (founding mood primal)

The top two magnitudes borrow directly from two of the founding mood primals, oi and au, while raseo takes the intensifier ra and the third primal, ai, is saved for fractions (below). Rather than coining new phonemes, the ladder reuses material the language already has, in keeping with the "compounding before coining" ethos. It also leaves a small joke sitting in plain sight: numbers big enough to reach oiseo and auseo are, appropriately, attached to alarm and pain.

3. Fractions & Decimals

The remaining founding mood primal, ai (desire / SEEKING), was repurposed for fractional quantities. A fitting reuse, since a fraction is a quantity reaching toward a whole it hasn't attained.

Ionnoioni distinguishes two registers for stating a fraction or decimal, matched to how precise the speaker needs to be:

Casual register

Numeral, the connective sne ("and"), ai ("toward"), numeral:

kan sne ai san "3 and toward 5", 3.5

Precise register

Numeral, sne, numeral, then a ladder-word marking exact place value:

kan sne san aiseo "3 and 5 tenths", 3.5 stated to the tenths place
PlaceWord
tenthsaiseo
hundredthsaiseseo
thousandthsaiseso

Both registers are correct Ionnoioni. The casual form is what a shopkeeper reaches for; the precise form is what an engineer needs when the exact place value matters.

4. The Time-Word Class

Time-words form their own sixth root class: not animate, not inanimate, not mood, not verb, not ideophone. They needed their own logic because time doesn't behave like an object, a feeling or an action.

Phenomenological anchoring Rather than treat time as an abstract unit, Ionnoioni names it by what's observably happening: dawn, morning, noon, evening, dusk, night. "Another morning" safely reads as "another day" from context alone.

Short units: ticking

Seconds, minutes and hours are felt as discrete, percussive beats, so they take a CrC shape with the trill sitting inside the word as connective glue rather than leading it:

Weeks, months and years are felt as looser, longer arcs, so they keep the trill leading (rC-), scaled by how fully each unit "encloses" experience:

The short and long units share the same three consonants (p/t/k) in the same connective-trill logic, just flipped in position: CrC for ticking, rC- for arcing.

5. Weekdays, Months & Dates

Weekdays

Numeral + rne, using the ordinary numerals 1–7:

WordDay
pan rneMonday
tan rneTuesday
kan rneWednesday
tos rneThursday
san rneFriday
ran rneSaturday
pon rneSunday

This deliberately overloads rne. It now means "morning," "day of the week" and (below) "day of the month." Context always disambiguates; this is consistent with how the language tolerates homophony elsewhere.

Months

The same pattern, using rke instead: numeral + rke, so month 9 (September) is kon i rke.

Day-of-month

Since Ionnoioni makes no ordinal/cardinal distinction, "the 10th" is simply seo, the same word as plain "10." Day-of-month reuses rne generically with the counting particle: seo i rne.

Putting it together

ton i krp, kan seo i trk. kan rne, seo i rne, kon i rke. tan seso tan seo ran 08:30, Wednesday the 10th of September, 2026

In the Siddham writing system, numerals stay in Devanagari digits, never spelled out, with the relevant classifier following in Siddham letters, matching spoken word order:

८𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖿𑖢𑖿, ३०𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖿𑖎𑖿 । ३𑖨𑖿𑖡𑖸, १०𑖨𑖿𑖡𑖸, ९𑖨𑖿𑖎𑖸 । २०२६𑖨𑖿𑖝𑖲 8-hour, 30-minute. 3-day(week), 10-day(month), 9-month. 2026-year

6. Measures

Rather than build weight, distance, volume and area words from scratch within the closed p/t/k/n/s/r inventory, Ionnoioni borrows the metric system wholesale, adapting pronunciation as needed. This is a deliberate exception to the language's usual closed-phoneme discipline: a living language absorbs loanwords, and metric units are about as close to a universal standard as anything in the modern world.

kren was chosen over the more obvious devoiced form kran because kran was already locked as the verb "allow." Loanwords still have to fit around the vocabulary that's already there.

Metric prefixes

Rather than invent a separate prefix system, Ionnoioni reuses the magnitude ladder and fraction ladder already built for numbers, fused directly onto the base unit word:

WordMeaning
sesoneterkilometer
aiseseonetercentimeter
aisesonetermillimeter

The same pattern extends automatically to the other base units with no new mechanism required: sesoriter/aiseseoriter/aisesoriter (kilo/centi/milliliter) and sesokren/aiseseokren/aisesokren (kilo/centi/milligram).

These forms fuse into a single word rather than staying separate, since the numeral accompanying them is always written as a Devanagari digit and already marks the count clearly. There's no risk of confusing the prefix with the unit itself.

३ 𑖭𑖸𑖭𑖺𑖡𑖸𑖝𑖸𑖨𑖿 kan sesoneter 3 kilometers
