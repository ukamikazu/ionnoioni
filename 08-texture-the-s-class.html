Texture: The S Class

Why 𑖭𑖿?

s is the only sibilant in Ionnoioni, and sibilants are arguably quite special. Whether it is the alarming hiss of a snake or the sound of a running rope as an anchor is dropped, it gets your attention. It adds texture and color, and even carries specific connotations depending on time, place and culture. s was, until recently, also the most rarely used leading sound in Ionnoioni's development. This is its moment to help highlight the subtleties of human being.

Mood names what is felt. The verb classes name what is done. Neither one names the grain of a thing, the quality of how it is felt or done, smooth or abrasive, fleeting or fixed, bounded or immersive. That is what the S class is for. Its words are not adjectives, adverbs or gerunds in the grammatical sense, Ionnoioni has none of those as dedicated parts of speech, but they function the way those categories function in other languages: they color an idea rather than naming a new one outright.

Three subclasses

The S class has three confirmed subclasses: pure color (sVsV), phenomenological color (s[verb]sV) and material color (s + vowel + an inanimate root). All three share the same governing idea: a vowel slot can carry the exact sense that vowel already holds elsewhere in the grammar. In the first two, the lead element grounds the word while the trailing element qualifies it, the same ordering tendency seen throughout the rest of the language. Material color deliberately turns that order around, as described in its own section below.

sVsV: pure, abstract color

No verb is embedded. The word is built entirely from two vowel slots, each carrying a particle-sense, bound together by s on both ends. This is free-floating texture, not tethered to any concrete action, closer to the grain of a relationship, a mood or a felt quality than to anything a body does.

The full sVsV grid

Eight vowel senses in the lead slot and eight in the trailing slot give sixty-four possible words, and every one of them has been tested by ear and judged to name something real and specific. The cells that resisted were resolved by stating a precise English phrase and checking it against the two vowel senses; more often than not, the hard-to-paraphrase meaning turned out to be nothing more than its vowel senses spelled out in full. That is good evidence the method does real semantic work rather than producing evocative noise.

A note on oi: alarm and fear are not quite the same thing

Alarm and fear are evolutionarily close but distinct: alarm is closer to an external signal, the utility of noticing and avoiding a threat, where fear is the internal fight, flight or freeze response that follows. oi, as a single root, legitimately spans both senses, because it sits at that old evolutionary fork, carrying something close to very old biological code in a single syllable. A speaker disambiguates by context, the same way real languages' fear and alarm vocabulary works. This matters for reading the S class: an oi-closing word can mean a warning is present (skaursoi, a feared death, or sprensoi, a confidence trickster, too good to be true) just as readily as it can mean the subject themselves feels afraid.

s[verb]sV: concrete, phenomenological color

A whole existing verb is embedded intact inside the s-frame: s + [verb] + s + [closing vowel]. The verb grounds the word's meaning; the closing vowel qualifies it with its own particle-sense, exactly as in sVsV. This subclass is tethered to something a body or mind can be caught doing, which makes it phenomenological where sVsV stays abstract.

The frame accommodates whatever shape the embedded verb actually has. Clean CCV verbs (pra, tro, kri, tre, pro, prai, kra, tri, tra, kro, tru, pre, kre, pri, pru, kru, prau) fit most naturally. CCVC verbs (prin, tron, prun, krun, tsor, kron, pron, pren, tran, kran, pran, trun, krak) fit too, producing a slightly heavier word. The two deliberately irregular death-cluster verbs, kaur (CVVC) and kreu (CCVV), embed just as readily with their own natural shape left intact, no truncation required. This was never a shape-restricted rule; it simply accommodates whichever verb shape already exists.

Verbs beginning with ts: a shared s

When the embedded verb already begins with ts, the frame's leading s is dropped and the verb's own s does the job. tsor (believe) gives tsorsa, tsorse and so on through tsorsoi, never stsorsa. Linguists call this kind of simplification haplology: two like sounds that would sit side by side collapse into one.

Using a texture word

A texture word works the way any predicate does in Ionnoioni. It can stand directly on a subject with no linking verb, it can take a direct object like a verb or attach what it is about with a relational particle, and it can stand as a noun in its own right.

𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿𑖨𑖰𑖡𑖿𑖭𑖺 𑖡 𑖌 𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸 𑖝𑖿𑖨𑖲𑖡𑖿
nio sprinso na o kre trun
I am shrinking away from pride (a predicate, with its complement attached by na o)
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖺𑖭𑖸 𑖝𑖿𑖃
nio skrose toi
I care about you (taking a direct object)
𑖡𑖰𑖌 𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖺𑖭𑖸 𑖌 𑖝𑖿𑖃
nio skrose o toi
My care goes out toward you (with a relational particle)
𑖝𑖰𑖌 𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖿𑖨𑖸𑖄𑖭
tio pra skreusa
You spoke your grief (as a noun, the object of pra)

Material color: the grain of things

The third subclass embeds a whole inanimate root after a single vowel: s + vowel + root. Every inanimate root begins with a stop and an s (ps-, ts- or ks-), so the shape falls out naturally: s + a + kso (fire) gives sakso. Where phenomenological color gives the texture of doing, material color gives the texture of things: stone and tools, trees and water, mind and fire.

The order is flipped. In every other S-class word the first element grounds the meaning and the second qualifies it. Here the root, the concrete part, grounds the word and the leading vowel qualifies it. Every word in this subclass is a kind of its root: sakso is a kind of fire, a hearth, not a kind of withness. It is the one place in the S class where the order turns around, and that is part of what marks it as a different scale of texture.

Bracketing a texture: ta and ra

The bracket particles work on texture words exactly as they work everywhere else. ta softens and ra intensifies, and where the particle stands says where that softening or intensity falls: before the word marks the onset, after it marks the resolution, and on both sides marks the whole thing throughout. That gives every S-class word six bracketed forms, three gentler and three stronger, generating a full spectrum from a single root without coining anything new.

ta and ra are never mixed in one form. An intense onset that gentles, or a soft beginning that builds, is already implied by where a single bracket stands; adding the opposite bracket would be superfluous at best and noise at worst.

Each texture can be laid out as a card: the word and its Siddham, its base reading, then the six bracketed forms.

Why this might matter more than it first appears

Every other expansion of this language's vocabulary, the mood primals, the kru- family, even the earlier sVsV words, required inventing or discovering one meaning at a time. s[verb]sV is different: once the rule is fixed, a word's meaning can be read off its components rather than separately invented. The expressive range multiplies by roughly the number of eligible verbs at a stroke, rather than growing one coinage at a time. It is a genuine answer to a problem every one of this project's vocabulary sessions has run into: feelings and textures that are difficult, or merely cumbersome, to express, made close to trivial to name once the pieces already exist.

Zero grade: textures of the beyond

A small set of S-class words deliberately breaks the rule that s stands on both sides of the first vowel. Each is a single s, a plain vowel and one of the three primals, with the second s left out. Their vowel senses read exactly as in sVsV, the first grounding and the second qualifying, but they reach toward a metaphysical or spiritual register: devotion, the sacred, the holy and the infinite.

The missing s is the point. It represents the incompleteness that makes these textures possible at all: each names a reaching toward something that is never fully grasped, held or arrived at, and the word itself is left open in the same way.

Lengthened grade: textures as worlds

The zero grade stops short of its second s and is left open toward what can never be reached. The lengthened grade does the opposite: the word runs on past its last vowel and gutters out in a final s, like a candle flame going out. That closing hiss seals the texture, and a sealed texture is no longer something felt between people. It has become a world: the climate, the structure or the culture that people live inside.

The names come from Indo-European linguistics, where roots appear in a zero grade, a full grade and a lengthened grade.

The lengthened grade is productive, like the ta and ra brackets. Any full-grade word in any of the three subclasses may take the final s wherever the scale-up makes sense, and speakers are free to coin them. Not every texture has a world to become: a single utterance (sprasa) probably does not. The zero grade never takes it, since those words are defined by the s they lack.

What is still open

  • All thirty-two verbs have been run through the s[verb]sV frame and all fifteen inanimate roots through material color. Only the verb compounds (trekri, trekra, trotri and tretra) remain unrun, and they may be best left as they are.
  • With sixty-four sVsV words, two hundred fifty-six verb-family words, one hundred twenty material-color words and fourteen in the zero grade, the S class now holds four hundred fifty-four textures. Every one of them now has its six bracketed forms laid out on the Texture Cards page.
  • spraiso and skronsa share an English gloss, anticipation. English simply runs short here. The Ionnoioni words differ in grain, and that difference is still waiting to be spelled out.
  • The shape sCVsV, and its lengthened form, is set aside, because its opening (sne, sre, ste, spe, ske) is already the shape of the connectives.
  • Only eight lengthened-grade words are established so far. The rest are left for speakers to find as they need them.
