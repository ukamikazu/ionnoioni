Oaths

Every language needs somewhere to put its frustration. Ionnoioni puts it in the fire.

How an oath is built

An oath is one of the five fire roots followed by a sound that means nothing at all. The roots are ordinary words for things that burn, and their sound already belongs to profanity: ks- has carried the color of an oath since the language began. The bare root can stand alone, as in kso!, the simplest oath there is.

RootSiddhamMeaningAs an oath
ksa𑖎𑖿𑖭embera smoldering annoyance
kse𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖸ashdisgust, something ruined
kso𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖺fireanger
ksi𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖰shroudheavy, taboo, the register of death
ksu𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖲place of burningthe strongest, the fire with nothing left to burn

The roots run roughly from mild to strong, but speakers will rank them however they like.

The noise

The second half of an oath is one of seven sounds left over from elsewhere in the language, sounds that were set aside because they were too close to other words or too rude in other languages. They mean nothing in Ionnoioni and never will.

pes 𑖢𑖸𑖭𑖿 · kas 𑖎𑖭𑖿 · kis 𑖎𑖰𑖭𑖿 · kos 𑖎𑖺𑖭𑖿 · pis 𑖢𑖰𑖭𑖿 · pus 𑖢𑖲𑖭𑖿 · pos 𑖢𑖺𑖭𑖿

Every one of these seven ends the way a color word ends, so swearing in Ionnoioni is, quite literally, colorful language.

Five more sounds joined them later: words that were set aside from the language's qualities because they sounded like English words, and because they end hard, on clusters the rest of the language avoids. That hardness suits an oath.

ant 𑖀𑖡𑖿𑖝𑖿 · ask 𑖀𑖭𑖿𑖎𑖿 · apt 𑖀𑖢𑖿𑖝𑖿 · asp 𑖀𑖭𑖿𑖢𑖿 · akt 𑖀𑖎𑖿𑖝𑖿

These begin with a vowel, so they meet the root's own vowel head on: ksoakt!, ksuapt! The language never doubles a vowel, so when ksa meets one of them the two a sounds fuse into one: ksa and ant make ksant! Five roots and twelve sounds make sixty oaths.

Where the meaning comes from

An oath carries no meaning of its own. It can be friendly, painful or a curse on someone's grandchildren, and the people saying it decide which. Knowing people, they will usually decide on the worst. Innocent noise is raised to execration with nothing more than the ordinary grammar and a little real fire:

  • nr offers it in trust, so the oath becomes affection.
  • ta softens it, for teasing. ra intensifies it, and on both sides it is meant all the way through.
  • tr marks it as meant. pr marks it as something that slipped out.
  • Oaths can be stacked, the smallest fire to the largest, and fast speech is free to wear a stack down into something new.

How an oath is delivered, the face and the pitch and the pause before it, is never written down. That part is always up to the speaker.

Examples

The examples below are hidden for your protection.

A mild one (open only if you can take the heat)
𑖢𑖿𑖨 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖎𑖭𑖿!
pr ksakas!
literally: involuntarily: ember, and a sound
What slips out when you stub your toe. pr marks it as unintended, so nobody holds it against you.
A friendly one (open only if you can take the heat)
𑖡𑖿𑖨, 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖺𑖎𑖰𑖭𑖿!
nr, ksokis!
literally: (gently, in trust) fire, and a sound
Said with a grin to an old friend who has done something outrageous. The nr makes it affection.
A teasing one (open only if you can take the heat)
𑖝 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖸𑖢𑖲𑖭𑖿 𑖝
ta ksepus ta
literally: ash, and a sound, softened all the way through
Ribbing someone you like. Nothing in it is meant to land.
A furious one (open only if you can take the heat)
𑖨 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖺𑖎𑖺𑖭𑖿 𑖨
ra ksokos ra
literally: fire, and a sound, intense all the way through
No longer a joke. The same root as the friendly one, with the heat turned all the way up.
A deliberate one (open only if you can take the heat)
𑖝𑖿𑖨 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖰𑖢𑖸𑖭𑖿!
tr ksipes!
literally: willingly: a shroud, and a sound
Every syllable meant. The shroud root carries the weight of death and taboo, and tr owns it.
The worst one (open only if you can take the heat)
𑖨 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖲𑖢𑖺𑖭𑖿 𑖨
ra ksupos ra
literally: the place of burning, and a sound, intense all the way through
The generational curse. Do not say it to anyone you hope to see again.
The triple decker (open only if you can take the heat)
𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖎𑖭𑖿 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖺𑖎𑖰𑖭𑖿 𑖎𑖿𑖭𑖲𑖢𑖺𑖭𑖿!
ksakas ksokis ksupos!
literally: ember and a sound, fire and a sound, the place of burning and a sound
Three oaths stacked from the smallest fire to the largest in one breath, for the speaker with a poet's ear and a grammarian's patience.
